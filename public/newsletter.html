03Myth, Busted

"If you're not sore the next day, the workout didn't work."

This is one of the things I hear most, and it's holding people back for no reason. Soreness just means your muscles did something unfamiliar, not that the session was effective. The more consistent you get with a movement, the less sore you'll feel doing it, even as you get stronger and fitter at it. Some of the best sessions I've coached leave people barely sore at all.

Why It Matters

Chasing soreness leads people to either overtrain trying to "earn" it, or feel like they've failed when a solid session doesn't leave them aching. Neither helps you progress. Consistency and steady improvement matter far more than how wrecked you feel the next day.

The takeaway: track your progress through what you can lift, how you feel, and whether you're showing up consistently, not how sore you are. If anything, less soreness over time is usually a sign you're adapting well.
